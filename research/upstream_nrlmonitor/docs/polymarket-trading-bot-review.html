00Review Verdict

Rev 1 is a strong piece of systems thinking with correct instincts on phasing, risk gating and separation of concerns. It is not yet safe to implement, because the financial mathematics contain errors that would silently mis-size every position, and the validation plan cannot detect whether the strategy works.

What Rev 1 gets right

  • Phase gating with go/no-go criteria. The instinct to survey markets before writing code, and to paper trade before funding, is the correct order of operations and is often skipped.
  • Naming the fundamental risk explicitly. Section 13 leads with "no edge may exist." Most trading-bot specifications bury this. It belongs at the front, and it does drive the phase design.
  • Recognising that PW confidence is not a probability. This is the single most common category error in model-to-market pipelines, and Rev 1 identifies it unprompted.
  • Stateless signal relay. The monitor knowing nothing about wallets or positions is a good boundary and should be preserved through the redesign.
  • Honesty about backfill contamination. Flagging that 15,215 of 15,477 calls carry synthetic odds, rather than quoting a headline ROI, is intellectually disciplined.

Recommended disposition

Do not proceed to Phase 2 as written. Insert a Phase 0 (data capture) that begins recording order-book snapshots and decision context immediately — that data cannot be reconstructed later and everything downstream depends on it. Re-issue Sections 7, 8 and 11 of Rev 1 with the corrections in this review, then resume.

01Critical Defects

Defects that would cause incorrect trading behaviour, misstate results, or invalidate the decision to deploy capital.

C-01

critical Kelly sizing gives two contradictory formulas

Section 7 presents the Kelly fraction as a single derivation across two lines, but the lines are not equal. The second is correct; the first is wrong by a factor of the market price.

f_kelly = edge / (1 / market_prob - 1)          # = edge * q / (1 - q)   WRONG
        = (calibrated_prob - market_prob) / (1 - market_prob)   # = edge / (1 - q)   CORRECT

At a price of $0.62 with an 11-point edge, the first line yields 0.180 and the second 0.289 — a 38% difference. Because the discrepancy is proportional to price, the error is not a constant scaling: a bot implementing line 1 would run at 0.31× Kelly on a 62¢ contract and 0.45× Kelly on a 90¢ contract, so the risk profile silently varies with entry price.

Fix: State one canonical form only. For a binary contract bought at cost c with true probability p, the growth-optimal fraction is f* = (p - c) / (1 - c). Derivation and fee-adjusted version in Section 06. Delete the alternate expression — ambiguity in a sizing formula is itself the defect.
C-02

critical Fee model is wrong in both magnitude and shape

Rev 1 states "Maker: 0%. Taker: ~2% of notional." Polymarket's fee schedule changed twice during 2026 and is not a flat percentage. The international taker fee follows the formula fee = shares × θ × price × (1 − price), which peaks at the 50¢ midpoint and falls toward both extremes. Sports carries θ = 0.05 as of July 2026, a maximum of $1.25 per 100 shares at 50¢, updated upward from θ = 0.03. Separately, sell orders are never charged a taker fee, and resting limit orders pay approximately zero and can earn a daily rebate.

Entry priceFee per shareAs % of notionalRev 1 assumption
$0.501.25¢2.50%2.0%
$0.621.18¢1.90%2.0%
$0.750.94¢1.25%2.0%
$0.850.64¢0.75%2.0%

The error runs in both directions and is largest exactly where PW fires most often. More importantly, the whole framing is wrong: fees should be handled in probability points, not percent of notional, because that is the unit edge is measured in. The fee is θ·p·(1−p) dollars per share, which subtracts directly from edge.

Fix: Implement the published formula with θ loaded from configuration and verified against the live schedule at start-up, not hard-coded. Subtract the fee from edge in probability space. Treat the schedule as volatile — it moved twice this year — and alert if the observed fee on a fill deviates from the predicted fee by more than a tolerance.
C-03

critical The paper-trading gate cannot detect whether the strategy works

Section 11 requires "≥ 50 games" and "simulated ROI > 0%". Betting returns are extremely high-variance and 50 samples carries almost no information. For a contract bought at $0.62 with a true win probability of 0.70, the standard deviation of return per unit staked is 0.739. Over 50 trades the standard error of measured ROI is 10.5 percentage points, so the 95% interval spans roughly ±21 points.

Put plainly: after 50 paper trades, a strategy with a true ROI of −8% and one with +12% produce indistinguishable results most of the time. The gate as written is close to a coin flip on whether real money gets deployed.

Fix: Two changes. First, keep ROI as a monitoring metric but demote it as a gate. Second, adopt closing-line value as the primary decision KPI — it is one to two orders of magnitude more sample-efficient because it measures a price difference rather than a binary outcome. Full treatment in Section 05.
C-04

critical Edge is evaluated against a price the bot cannot actually get

Section 6 step 4 fetches "best ask price for YES token" and Section 7 computes edge against it. Best ask is the price of the first share only. A $50 order at 62¢ is roughly 80 shares; if the top level holds 25 shares and the next two levels sit at 63¢ and 65¢, the realised average cost is materially above the quoted ask. In thin WNBA books the divergence will be much larger, and the liquidity guard in Section 8 ("ensure sufficient depth") is stated as an intention with no formula attached.

The consequence is systematic overstatement of edge on precisely the trades where edge is thinnest — an adverse-selection pattern that biases the whole P&L record upward during paper trading and then disappears in live results.

Fix: Compute a size-weighted sweep price across the visible book to the intended order size, and evaluate edge against that. If the sweep cannot be filled within the slippage cap, reduce size until it can, or skip. Implementation in Section 06.
C-05

critical Game-date handling has an off-by-one-day defect

The example payload in Section 5 is internally inconsistent and demonstrates the bug:

"game_date": "2026-11-15",
"ts":         "2026-11-15T02:30:00Z"    <-- 21:30 ET on Nov 14

A signal firing at 02:30 UTC during a game belongs to the US venue date of 14 November, not 15 November. Since the market slug is built from game_date, every evening US game — which is nearly all of them — would resolve to a slug one day in the future and return no market. On an AU-hosted monitor at UTC+10/11 the local date is wrong in the other direction. Roughly 90% of the NBA slate is affected.

Fix: Never derive game date from wall-clock time on any host. Carry the scheduled date in the venue's local timezone from the schedule source through the payload, and set every process to UTC internally with explicit conversion at the boundary. Better still, remove date arithmetic from the resolution path entirely (Section 08).
C-06

critical Every hosting option in Rev 1 is in a blocked jurisdiction

Section 9 evaluates three options — Oracle Cloud Free Singapore, Vultr Singapore 4GB, Vultr Singapore 2GB — and recommends Singapore as the VPS location. Section 10's geo table lists Singapore as accessible and not on the restricted list.

Singapore has blocked Polymarket since 12 January 2025. The Gambling Regulatory Authority ordered ISPs to implement DNS-level blocking, classifying the platform as an illegal gambling site under the Gambling Control Act 2022, and this was reported by Bloomberg and others at the time. Some restriction trackers now list Singapore under close-only status, meaning no new positions. The GRA's own notice is framed as covering unlicensed remote gambling provided "in or from Singapore" — language that speaks to activity originating there, not just to consumers receiving it.

Rev 1's hosting section is therefore not merely sub-optimal; all three options fail outright, and the recommendation has been wrong for eighteen months. This is the clearest illustration of the point made in Section 04: platform and regulatory facts in this space age fast and must be verified rather than carried forward.

Fix: Discard the Singapore options entirely. Revised hosting analysis in Section 16, which also accounts for the corrected operator jurisdiction. Add a start-up check that asserts the deployment region is not on the current restricted list, and re-check it on a schedule rather than at design time.

02High-Severity Defects

H-01

high The min_ev gate can never fire

Expected value per dollar staked on a binary contract is (p − q) / q, which is exactly edge / price. With min_edge = 0.08 and max_price = 0.90, the lowest EV any passing trade can have is 0.08 / 0.90 = 0.089, already above the 0.05 threshold. The gate is unreachable configuration — it looks like a safety control on the config page and does nothing.

Fix: Remove one of the two gates. Prefer keeping EV as the primary gate (it is the economically meaningful quantity and correctly penalises expensive contracts) and derive the edge floor from it, or keep edge and delete min_ev. Add a start-up assertion that no configured gate is dominated by another.
H-02

high The webhook signature scheme is circular and replayable

Section 5 places "hmac": "sha256=..." inside the JSON body while also stating the HMAC covers the full JSON body. A payload cannot contain a digest of itself. Beyond the contradiction, the scheme has no replay protection: a captured request can be resent indefinitely, and while the bot's (game_id, predicted_team) dedup limits the damage, the dedup cache is the only thing standing between a replayed packet and an order.

Fix: Move the signature to the X-Signature header and sign the exact raw bytes received, not a re-serialised object. Include the timestamp in the signed material, reject anything outside a ±120s window, keep a short-lived nonce cache, and compare with hmac.compare_digest. Require NTP on both hosts. Better again: eliminate the inbound endpoint entirely (Section 09).
H-03

high Always-crossing the spread pays a fee that is avoidable

Section 6 step 7 places a limit buy at "best ask + $0.01". That is a marketable order: it pays the taker fee, plus a cent of deliberate price concession, and forfeits the maker rebate. On the document's own 8-point minimum edge, the combined cost is roughly 2.2 points — more than a quarter of the entire edge, given away by execution policy rather than lost to the market.

Fix: A maker-first ladder. Post inside the spread, reprice on a short timer, and cross only if the edge still clears the gate after the taker fee is subtracted. Full policy in Section 07. This is the highest-value change in the review relative to effort.
H-04

high The headline contamination statistic is self-contradictory

Section 1 reads: "98% of NBA PW calls (262 of 15,477) have live-priced bookmaker odds. The remaining 98% are backfill-derived." 262 of 15,477 is 1.7%, and the summary panel correctly says 2%. The first figure should read 2%.

This matters more than a typo: it is the single number that determines how much weight the historical ROI deserves, and it appears in the executive summary where it is most likely to be quoted onward.

Fix: Correct to "2% of NBA PW calls (262 of 15,477) have live-priced bookmaker odds; the remaining 98% are backfill-derived." Add the corresponding WNBA counts rather than the bare 37%.
H-05

high No reconciliation between internal state and exchange truth

Section 8 says the state file persists open positions and systemd restarts the process. Nothing verifies that the state file matches reality. Every failure mode that matters lives in that gap: an order submitted but not recorded before a crash, a partial fill, a manual intervention, a settlement the bot missed while offline, a position that exists on-chain but not in the ledger.

There is one genuinely reassuring property Rev 1 never states explicitly and should: positions are on-chain tokens held by the wallet, so losing the VPS does not lose the position — it settles regardless. The exposure is management, not custody. That deserves to be written down, because it means aggressive recovery behaviour is safe.

Fix: A reconciliation loop that runs on boot and on a timer: pull positions and trade history from the CLOB, pull USDC balance from chain, diff against the internal ledger, and refuse to trade while a discrepancy is unresolved. Reconcile-before-trade on start-up is non-negotiable for a system that can restart mid-slate.
H-06

high The drift auto-kill fires spuriously about a quarter of the time

Section 13 proposes "auto-kill if rolling 20-trade accuracy drops below 55%". If the model's true accuracy is 65%, the probability of observing 11 or fewer wins in 20 trades is roughly 24%. The control will halt a perfectly healthy system on nearly one window in four, which trains the operator to override it — the classic failure path for automated safety controls.

Fix: Replace threshold-on-a-short-window with a sequential change detector. A CUSUM on per-trade Brier score, or a sequential probability ratio test against the null "calibration unchanged", gives a tunable false-alarm rate and detects real degradation faster than a fixed window. Keep a hard kill for catastrophic thresholds, but set it where a false positive is genuinely unlikely.
H-07

high Consensus taxonomy is inconsistent across three sections

Section 2 lists consensus values as strong, ml_only, historical_only, conflicted. The payload in Section 5 carries blended as a separate boolean. Section 7 then assigns a multiplier to a consensus value called blended. Meanwhile the relay filter defaults to ["strong","blended"], so the ml_only and historical_only multipliers in Section 7 can never be exercised — those signals are dropped before they leave the monitor.

Fix: Publish one enumerated schema for the payload, validate it on both sides, and reject unknown values loudly rather than silently defaulting. Decide deliberately whether ml_only and historical_only are traded at reduced size or filtered out, and make the two sections agree.
H-08

high No exit logic, despite live markets and fee-free sells

The architecture diagram lists "auto-exit at game end", the failure table says positions are held to settlement, and Section 13 raises selling as an open question. In fact Polymarket supports trading on live sports in real time, and sell orders are not charged a taker fee — the fee applies on entry, not exit.

That asymmetry is a gift and Rev 1 leaves it on the table. Exits are the cheapest action available to this system. Holding a position that has run to 95¢ ties up capital for the remaining nickel while a fresh signal with a 10-point edge goes unfunded, and holding a position after the model's premise has broken is a pure loss.

Fix: Specify an exit policy as a first-class component: exit on model invalidation, on price reaching a configured fraction of fair value, and on capital pressure when a better-edged signal arrives. Requires the continuous fair-value model in Section 13.
H-09

high A synchronous 5-second POST sits inside the 30-second poll loop

Section 5 describes fire-and-forget with a 5s timeout, and separately requires that it "must not block monitor cycle". Both cannot be true as specified: a synchronous request with a 5s timeout blocks for up to 5s. With several signals per cycle on a busy slate, a slow or unreachable bot can consume a large fraction of the cycle budget and delay the monitor's primary job.

Fix: The monitor writes to a local durable outbox and returns immediately. A separate worker delivers with retry, backoff and jitter. Delivery latency is then decoupled from the poll loop entirely, and signals survive a bot outage instead of being dropped.
H-10

high JSON state file is not crash-safe

A trading ledger held in a JSON file rewritten in place will eventually be truncated by a crash or power loss mid-write, and there is no transaction boundary around "order submitted" and "position recorded". The document's own failure table assumes the file survives a crash; nothing in the design makes that true.

Fix: SQLite in WAL mode with real transactions, or Postgres if the deployment consolidates. Append-only event table as the source of truth, derived state as projections. Atomic writes and a backup on every slate boundary.
H-11

high Concurrent positions are sized as if independent

Kelly is derived for sequential, independent bets. Ten simultaneous NBA positions on one evening's slate are none of those things: they share a model, a feature pipeline, a calibration curve and a market regime. If the model is miscalibrated tonight, it is miscalibrated on all ten. Sizing each at half-Kelly independently produces total risk well above the intended half-Kelly.

Fix: Size against a slate-level budget rather than per-game caps alone. A defensible simple rule is to scale each position by 1/√n in the expected number of concurrent positions, with a hard slate exposure cap. The principled version is a portfolio optimisation over the joint outcome distribution (Section 13).

05The Measurement Problem

The most consequential weakness in Rev 1 is not a coding error. It is that the plan cannot answer its own central question — does this edge exist? — within any realistic number of trades.

Why 50 trades tells you nothing

For a contract bought at price q with true win probability p, the return per unit staked is (1−q)/q on a win and −1 on a loss. At q = 0.62, p = 0.70 the mean return is 12.9% and the standard deviation is 0.739. The minimum ROI detectable at 80% power with a one-sided 5% test scales as 2.49 × σ / √n:

TradesStd. error of ROISmallest detectable true ROIVerdict
5010.5 pp26.0%Uninformative
1007.4 pp18.4%Uninformative
2504.7 pp11.6%Weak
5003.3 pp8.2%Weak
1,0002.3 pp5.8%Marginal
2,0001.7 pp4.1%Usable
4,0001.2 pp2.9%Good

A realistic post-fee edge for this kind of system is in the low single digits. Detecting a 5% true ROI takes roughly 1,350 trades. At three to eight PW calls per game, deduplicated to one trade per game, and a fraction of games passing the edge gate, that is one to three full seasons. ROI is not a viable primary gate on any timeline the project cares about.

The alternative: closing-line value

Closing-line value measures the price you paid against the price the market settled at just before resolution. It works because the closing price is the market's most informed estimate, so beating it consistently is direct evidence of informational edge — and because it is a price difference rather than a binary outcome, its variance is one to two orders of magnitude smaller.

Empirically CLV per bet has a standard deviation of a few percentage points rather than 74. Detecting a mean CLV of +1pp at 80% power takes on the order of 50–100 observations rather than 1,350. That brings validation inside a single paper-trading phase.

Recommended KPI hierarchy

  1. Primary gate: mean CLV > 0 with a confidence interval excluding zero. Measured as entry price versus the last traded price before resolution, on the same token.
  2. Secondary gate: calibration. Reliability of the calibrated probability against outcomes, with reported standard error, not a bare ECE threshold on 50 samples.
  3. Monitoring only: ROI and hit rate. Tracked and reported, never used as a go/no-go before four figures of trades.
  4. Diagnostic: adverse selection. Price movement in the 1, 5 and 30 minutes after each fill. Consistent movement against you after entry means the counterparty knew more, and is the earliest warning that the edge is illusory.

One caution: CLV on in-play markets is subtler than pre-game CLV because the fair value moves with the game state, not just with information. Measure CLV against the price at a fixed horizon after entry as well as at close, and interpret both.

Pre-register the analysis

With this many dimensions available for slicing — quarter, consensus type, price band, league, margin, day of week — some subset will look profitable by chance. Decide in advance which segmentation defines success and how many hypotheses are being tested, and hold to it. Otherwise Phase 3 will find an edge whether or not one exists.

06Corrected Edge & Sizing Model

Replacement mathematics for Section 7 of Rev 1, with fees, book depth and estimation uncertainty handled explicitly.

Rev 1
market_prob = polymarket_ask_price
edge = calibrated_prob - market_prob
ev = calibrated_prob * (1/market_prob - 1)
     - (1 - calibrated_prob)

f_kelly = edge / (1/market_prob - 1)
f_half  = f_kelly / 2
position_usd = bankroll * f_half
Rev 2
cost = sweep_price(book, shares)
       + taker_fee(price, theta)      # 0 if maker
edge = p_lower - cost                 # p_lower is
                                      # uncertainty-adjusted
ev   = edge / cost

f    = (p_lower - cost) / (1 - cost)
size = bankroll_slate_start
       * f * KELLY_FRAC * consensus_mult
       / sqrt(expected_concurrent)

Fees in probability space

SPORTS_THETA = 0.05        # intl schedule, sports, Jul 2026. Load from config; verify at runtime.

def taker_fee_per_share(price: float, theta: float = SPORTS_THETA) -> float:
    """Dollars per share. Charged on BUY only -- SELL is fee-free.
    Denominated per share, so it subtracts directly from edge in probability points."""
    return theta * price * (1.0 - price)

def effective_cost(price: float, is_taker: bool, theta: float = SPORTS_THETA) -> float:
    return price + (taker_fee_per_share(price, theta) if is_taker else 0.0)

Depth-weighted entry price

def sweep_price(asks, shares_wanted):
    """asks: [(price, size), ...] ascending by price.
    Returns (vwap, shares_fillable, worst_level). Never assume top-of-book is the fill."""
    remaining, notional, worst = shares_wanted, 0.0, None
    for price, available in asks:
        take = min(remaining, available)
        notional += take * price
        worst = price
        remaining -= take
        if remaining <= 0:
            return notional / shares_wanted, shares_wanted, worst
    filled = shares_wanted - remaining
    return (notional / filled if filled else None), filled, worst

If the sweep cannot fill the intended size within the slippage cap, reduce size until it can rather than skipping outright — a smaller position at good prices beats no position, and beats a full position at bad ones.

Best execution across the complementary token

A moneyline market has two outcome tokens that sum to $1. Buying team A is economically identical to selling team B, and the two books do not always agree. Checking both routinely picks up half a cent to a cent, which on an 8-point edge is worth having.

def best_entry(book_a, book_b):
    """Buying YES_A == selling YES_B. Compare the direct ask against the synthetic route."""
    direct    = book_a.best_ask
    synthetic = 1.0 - book_b.best_bid
    return ("direct", direct) if direct <= synthetic else ("synthetic_via_b", synthetic)

Canonical Kelly, with uncertainty

def kelly_fraction(p: float, cost: float) -> float:
    """Growth-optimal fraction for a binary contract.
    Derivation: maximise p*ln(1 + f*(1-c)/c) + (1-p)*ln(1 - f)  ->  f* = (p - c)/(1 - c).
    ONE canonical form. Do not reintroduce alternates."""
    if not 0.0 < cost < 1.0:
        raise ValueError(f"cost out of range: {cost}")
    return max(0.0, (p - cost) / (1.0 - cost))


def position_size(p_hat, se_p, cost, cfg, bankroll, n_concurrent, consensus):
    # Size on a lower bound of the probability estimate, not the point estimate.
    # With a young calibration curve se_p is large and this correctly shrinks size toward zero.
    p_lower = p_hat - cfg.uncertainty_k * se_p

    f = kelly_fraction(p_lower, cost)
    f *= cfg.kelly_fraction                      # 0.5 = half-Kelly
    f *= cfg.consensus_multiplier[consensus]
    f /= max(1.0, n_concurrent) ** 0.5           # correlation haircut (H-11)

    return min(
        bankroll * f,
        cfg.max_bet_per_game,
        cfg.max_slate_exposure - open_slate_exposure(),
        cfg.max_total_exposure - open_total_exposure(),
    )

The uncertainty term is the point, not a refinement

Half-Kelly applied to a confidently wrong probability is still ruinous; the fraction protects against variance, not against bias. Sizing on p_hat − k·se means a calibration curve fitted on 50 samples, where the standard error is around 7 points, will wipe out most 8-point edges and size almost nothing. That is the correct behaviour. The system should start out barely able to trade and earn its way to size as the evidence accumulates.

A structural tension worth confronting early

Average PW confidence at fire is around 72%. For an 8-point edge to exist, Polymarket must be pricing the same team at or below 64¢. But PW fires on teams that are already ahead — the payload example shows an 8-point lead in Q2 — and a live market will typically have that team well above 64¢ already. The edge and the signal are pulling in opposite directions.

This is open question 2 in Rev 1, and it is the question. Phase 1 should not merely survey liquidity; it should join historical PW fire timestamps to historical token prices and produce the distribution of market_price_at_fire − pw_confidence. That analysis needs no capital, no bot and no VPS, and it can be done this week from the price-history endpoint. If the distribution is centred at or below zero, the project stops there and a great deal of engineering is saved.

Rev 1 crosses the spread on every entry. Given the current fee schedule, execution policy is one of the largest controllable inputs to returns.

Maker-first ladder

PLAN = [
    # (offset from best bid, seconds to rest, role)
    (+0.00, 8,  "maker"),   # join the bid
    (+0.01, 8,  "maker"),   # improve one tick
    (+0.02, 6,  "maker"),   # improve again, still passive
    ("cross", 0, "taker"),  # take, only if edge still clears post-fee
]

for offset, rest, role in PLAN:
    if not edge_still_clears(role):     # re-price the book, re-check the gate every step
        return abandon("edge decayed")
    place_or_amend(offset)
    if wait_for_fill(rest):
        return filled()
return abandon("unfilled")

The critical property is that the gate is re-evaluated at every rung. The taker fee is only incurred at the last step and only if the trade is still worth doing once it is charged. Partial fills are kept, not cancelled — a half-filled position at a good price is a good outcome.

Design notes

  • Rest times must be tuned against edge decay, not guessed. Phase 3 should measure how fast the price moves after a PW fire; if the market repricess in under ten seconds, patient posting loses more to decay than it saves in fees, and the ladder should compress. This is an empirical question the paper phase can answer directly.
  • Cancel/replace has a rate-limit cost. Amend rather than cancel-and-repost where the API supports it, and cap repricing frequency.
  • Exits go through the same ladder without the fee branch, since sells are not charged. Exit orders can be more patient.
  • Log the counterfactual. For every fill, record what an immediate cross would have cost. This produces a direct measurement of the execution policy's value and settles the question empirically within a few dozen trades.

A caution on maker orders

Resting orders get filled preferentially when the market is moving against you — that is what adverse selection means. A maker-first policy will show excellent average fill prices and a worse fill-conditional win rate than a taker policy. Both effects are real and they partly offset. The adverse-selection diagnostic in Section 05 exists precisely to measure the net, and it should be reported alongside execution savings rather than the savings being claimed alone.

08Market Resolution Redesign

String-built slugs are the wrong mechanism, and the failure mode is silent: an unmatched slug looks identical to a game with no market.

Why the current approach breaks

  • Naming variance: LA Clippers versus Los Angeles Clippers; Trail Blazers hyphenation; the leading digit in 76ers; WNBA expansion sides such as Toronto Tempo and Portland Fire that post-date any hard-coded map.
  • Date arithmetic: the off-by-one defect in C-05, plus doubleheaders and rescheduled games.
  • Neutral sites and specials: international games, in-season tournament fixtures and play-in games frequently carry different naming.
  • Silent failure: Rev 1 logs a warning and skips. On a bad slate that could be most of the card, and the bot would look healthy.

Recommended resolver

Resolution chain, first match wins, every step logged:

  1. Cached mapping    game_id -> (condition_id, token_ids)     # persisted, survives restart
  2. Structured lookup Gamma /sports + series + tag_id, filtered to
                       the venue-local date window, matched on the team pair
                       via a persisted team-ID map (ESPN id -> Polymarket team)
  3. Fuzzy fallback    normalised team-name match within the date window,
                       accepted only above a similarity threshold
  4. Unresolved        -> ALERT, record for manual mapping, never silently skip

Two properties matter more than the mechanism. First, pre-resolve the whole slate before games start — typically six hours ahead — so that resolution latency is entirely off the critical path when a signal fires and any unmatched game is surfaced while there is still time to fix it by hand. Second, track the unresolved rate as a first-class metric. A resolver that silently degrades from 95% to 60% coverage is indistinguishable from a quiet week unless somebody is counting.

The persisted team map should be seeded once from the Gamma teams endpoint and then treated as data, not code, so a new expansion franchise is a config change rather than a deploy.

09Revised Architecture

The two-node split is right. The transport, the internal decomposition and the storage layer all need reworking.

AU Server (monitor) Remote VPS (trading bot) ┌───────────────────────────┐ ┌──────────────────────────────────┐ │ PW call fires │ │ INGEST validate, dedupe, │ │ ├─ write to signal outbox │ bot pulls, with │ persist Signal event │ │ └─ return immediately (<1ms) │◄──cursor + backoff───├─────────────────────────────────│ │ │ │ DECIDE pure function: │ │ GET /signals?since=<cursor> │ │ (signal, book, cfg, │ │ signed, Tailscale-only │ │ calib) -> Decision │ └───────────────────────────┘ ├──────────────────────────────────│ │ EXECUTE maker ladder, order │ No inbound port on the bot. │ lifecycle, reconcile │ Replay is free: rewind the cursor. ├──────────────────────────────────│ Outage loses nothing. │ ACCOUNT settlement, P&L, CLV, │ │ chain reconciliation │ └──────────────────────────────────┘ │ ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐ │ SQLite (WAL): append-only event log │ │ + projections + L2 book snapshots │ └─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Three structural changes

1. Invert the transport: the bot pulls

Rev 1 has the monitor push to an internet-facing HTTPS endpoint on the bot. Inverting it so the bot polls a signed, cursor-based feed on the monitor is strictly better on four dimensions: no inbound port on the machine holding the wallet, at-least-once delivery with no bespoke retry logic, free replay by rewinding the cursor, and no signal loss during a bot outage. The monitor's write becomes a local append that returns in microseconds, which also fixes H-09.

If push is preferred for latency, keep it but back it with the outbox and a reconciling pull, so the push is an optimisation rather than the delivery guarantee.

2. Separate decide from execute

Rev 1 describes eight components but a single linear pipeline. Splitting the decision into a pure function — (signal, book snapshot, config, calibration) → Decision, no I/O, no clock, no randomness — buys an enormous amount:

  • Every decision is unit-testable against a fixture.
  • Every historical day can be replayed under different parameters, exactly, without touching the network.
  • Paper mode and live mode run identical decision code, so paper results actually predict live behaviour. In Rev 1 they are separate paths, which is how paper-to-live discrepancies get born.
  • Champion/challenger becomes trivial: run N configurations over the same snapshot stream.

3. Event-sourced storage

An append-only event log (SignalReceived, MarketResolved, DecisionMade, OrderSubmitted, OrderFilled, PositionSettled, ReconciliationRun) with derived projections gives the audit trail design principle 5 asks for, as a property of the storage model rather than as a logging convention that drifts. Each DecisionMade event stores the full input snapshot including the L2 book, the calibration model version and a hash of the config, so any trade can be explained months later.

Risks Rev 1 does not name

  • Resolution and oracle risk. Outcomes are settled by an oracle process that can be disputed or delayed. A correct prediction can still fail to pay on time. Hold a reserve and exclude disputed markets from performance statistics rather than booking them as wins.
  • Platform custody risk. Rev 1 covers wallet compromise but not the simpler case of funds being inaccessible — account restriction, withdrawal delay, platform outage during settlement. The profit sweep above is the mitigation.
  • Overfitting to the paper phase. If parameters are tuned against the same 50 games used to validate, the validation is meaningless. Hold out a segment of the paper period and do not look at it until the parameters are frozen.
  • Correlated model failure across leagues. NBA and WNBA share a pipeline. A bug in feature construction hits both simultaneously, so league diversification provides less protection than the exposure split implies.
  • Operator override risk. Every automated trading system eventually has a night where the operator disables a control that is behaving correctly. Make overrides logged, time-boxed and auto-expiring rather than a config toggle that stays flipped.

13New Capabilities & ROI Levers

Ordered by expected value per unit of effort. The first tier costs little and should begin before any trading code is written.

Tier 1 — Do these first

tier 1 Record everything, starting now

L2 order-book snapshots for every game on the slate, at fixed intervals and on every PW fire, plus the full decision context. This data cannot be reconstructed later and every subsequent capability depends on it: backtesting, replay, parameter tuning, execution analysis, adverse-selection measurement.

Zero risk, no capital, no trading. Should start before Phase 2. If nothing else in this review is adopted, adopt this.

tier 1 Closing-line value tracking

Entry price against the last traded price before resolution, per position. Becomes the primary decision KPI (Section 05) and shortens the evaluation loop from thousands of trades to dozens.

Requires only the price-history endpoint and the trade log. A day's work for the single largest improvement in decision quality.

tier 1 Devigged bookmaker consensus as an independent estimate

The monitor already carries bookmaker moneylines. Removing the overround — via Shin's method or a multiplicative or power devig, chosen empirically — yields an independent probability estimate that is generally well calibrated. Three estimates then exist for every game: PW, Polymarket, and the sportsbook consensus.

The filter that follows is powerful: only trade when PW disagrees with Polymarket and the devigged consensus supports PW. When PW disagrees with both, PW is far more likely to be wrong than to be early. This should remove a large fraction of false positives at essentially no cost.

Uses data already flowing through the monitor. Also gives a free sanity check on PW calibration that does not depend on the paper-trading sample at all.

tier 1 Maker-first execution and complementary-token routing

Sections 07 and 06. Recovers roughly a third of the edge that Rev 1's execution policy gives away, and applies to every trade the system ever makes.

Tier 2 — Model quality

tier 2 Stack PW with the market rather than competing against it

Instead of treating the Polymarket price as the adversary, treat it as a strong prior and PW as an update to it:

logit(p_final) = w0 + w1*logit(p_pw) + w2*logit(p_market) + w3*logit(p_devig)

Fitted on historical data with proper time-series validation, a stack of this form almost always dominates any single input. The weight w1 is then a direct, interpretable measurement of how much information PW carries beyond the market — which is the project's central question expressed as a single number. If w1 shrinks to zero in fitting, that is the answer, obtained without risking anything.

Requires historical price data joined to PW fires. Same input as the Phase 1 analysis recommended in Section 06.

tier 2 Continuous live win-probability model

PW fires discretely; a continuous fair value is more useful. A calibrated live model over score margin, time remaining, possession, pace and pre-game team strength gives a fair value at every moment, which enables edge monitoring between fires, principled exits, hedge sizing, and a much better calibration target than raw PW confidence.

The classical functional form — logistic in margin scaled by remaining time, anchored to a pre-game prior — is well understood, trains on play-by-play the monitor can already source, and is a stronger baseline than most bespoke models. It is also the prerequisite for the exit logic in H-08.

tier 2 Player availability and lineup pipeline

Late scratches and lineup confirmations are where genuine, legal informational edge in basketball actually lives, and prices move fastest on news that changes who is playing — confirmed lineups, injury updates and suspensions. A structured feed of official injury-report timings and starting-lineup confirmations, with a fast reaction path, is a more defensible edge than anything derived from score state, because score state is visible to every participant simultaneously while report timing is not.

Worth prioritising above further PW refinement. It is a different kind of edge, and it is uncorrelated with the model risk in the rest of the system.

tier 2 Context features and regime detection

Rest days and back-to-backs, travel and time-zone shifts, pace and style matchups, referee assignment. Separately, detect regimes where the model's assumptions break — garbage time, foul trouble on a key player, blowouts — and suppress trading rather than trying to model them.

Knowing when not to trade is usually worth more than a marginal accuracy gain, and it is much easier to validate.

Tier 4 — Research infrastructure

  • Replay harness. Deterministic re-run of any historical day under different parameters. Made possible by the pure decision function in Section 09 and the snapshots in Tier 1.
  • Champion/challenger shadow mode. Several strategy variants evaluated in parallel against live prices while at most one trades. New ideas earn their way to capital instead of being switched on.
  • Walk-forward validation with purged and embargoed splits. The backfill contamination Rev 1 flags is a leakage symptom; standard random cross-validation on this data will produce flattering nonsense.
  • Model registry and feature store. Every decision records model version, calibration version and config hash, so a result from three months ago can still be explained.
  • Analysis dashboard. Equity curve with drawdown, CLV distribution, reliability diagram, edge distribution, P&L attribution by quarter, consensus, price band and league, and execution quality. Attribution is what turns a losing month into a fixable diagnosis.

If only three things are built

Record order-book snapshots and decision context from today. Track closing-line value as the primary KPI. Run the historical join of PW fire times against Polymarket prices to see whether the edge exists at all. All three are cheap, none requires capital or a VPS, and together they answer the project's central question far faster and more cheaply than the five-phase plan does.

16Legal & Capital Position

Corrected premise: an Australian resident, temporarily in Malaysia

This section has now been revised twice. The first draft assumed an Australian operator; a correction indicated Malaysia; the refinement is that the operator is Australian, residing temporarily in Malaysia. That distinction is not minor, because the term that governs is residence, not location, and the two have come apart here.

Polymarket's terms of service are reported to state that the services are not available to persons who reside in, are located in, are incorporated in, or have a registered office in the United States or any prohibited locality. The limbs are disjunctive: satisfying one is enough to be excluded. Physical presence in Malaysia addresses "located in". It does not address "reside in" if residence remains Australian — and Australia is on the restricted list.

The word "temporarily" is doing the work here. Someone temporarily in a place ordinarily resides somewhere else.

This review will not offer ways to characterise the residence question more favourably or to structure the account around it — that is precisely the circumvention that Rev 1 correctly ruled out when it rejected VPN use, and it does not become different when the mechanism is residency framing rather than a tunnel.

This is now the gating item, ahead of the technical work in the roadmap rather than alongside it. The question for an Australian lawyer is narrow and answerable: given Australian residence and a temporary posting abroad, does this platform's eligibility position permit an account, and does the ACMA directive bear on it? Ask it before Phase 2, not before Phase 4 — there is no sense building a webhook relay for a system that cannot lawfully place an order.

Malaysia's own position, for completeness

If residence were to shift genuinely and permanently, Malaysia's position would still be grey rather than green. It is not on the block list, but no Malaysian regulator has classified prediction markets; the Common Gaming Houses Act 1953 and Betting Act 1953 are broad; MCMC has blocked several thousand gambling domains since 2022; amendments discussed through 2025–2026 would raise penalties for individual participants; Syariah law is a separate track for Muslim citizens; and Singapore, Thailand and Taiwan have all classified the platform as unlawful gambling. Rev 1's green cell describes access, not legality.

The licensed alternative: Betfair Exchange

Rev 1 treats Polymarket as the only venue, which is why the whole document ends up wrestling with jurisdiction. It is worth stating that a licensed exchange exists in the operator's own jurisdiction and would make the entire problem disappear.

Betfair Exchange is licensed in Australia and requires proof of Australian residency to register. It runs a back/lay exchange that is structurally close to Polymarket's binary contracts, offers in-play markets, and publishes a documented automation API with REST and streaming access, subject to a separate application and approval step. For an Australian resident, the eligibility question in this section simply does not arise.

Cost comparison in probability points

Betfair charges commission on net winnings per market rather than a fee on entry, so a losing market costs nothing extra. Converting to the break-even probability makes the two directly comparable: for a back bet at decimal odds d with commission c, break-even is 1 / [1 + (d−1)(1−c)]. Sport markets are published at a 6% base rate, though rates vary by market — NRL sits at 10% — so confirm the rate for basketball rather than assuming.

Against Polymarket as a taker, Betfair costs roughly 20–25% more. Against Polymarket as a maker, it costs infinitely more, because there is no zero-fee side. That is the real economic difference, and it is worth being clear that the maker-first ladder in Section 07 — the single highest-value execution recommendation in this review — loses half its purpose on Betfair. Posting passively still captures spread, which is worth having; it no longer eliminates a fee, because there is no entry fee to eliminate.

What transfers unchanged

Most of this review is venue-independent. The corrected Kelly formula applies with cost set to the break-even probability above. Depth-weighted pricing applies directly — Betfair exposes amounts available at each price, so the sweep logic in Section 06 is the same function against a different book shape. Calibration, model stacking, the devigged consensus filter, the sample-size mathematics, the event-sourced architecture, reconciliation, and every risk control in Section 11 all carry over without modification.

Closing-line value transfers particularly well. Betfair's closing price is the standard benchmark in the sports betting literature precisely because it is a liquid exchange price, so the primary KPI recommended in Section 05 is arguably better supported there than on Polymarket.

New risks specific to Betfair

  • Premium Charges. Betfair's published terms provide for additional charges on customers who, over the long term, generate little commission relative to the amount they win. This is a success penalty with no Polymarket equivalent: if the bot works, it may become more expensive. Read the current terms carefully before sizing up — this materially changes the economics of a genuinely profitable strategy, which is the only scenario worth planning for.
  • In-play suspensions. Exchanges suspend markets around significant in-game events, and unmatched orders can be cancelled. An automated system must handle suspension, re-match and cancellation states explicitly rather than assuming a continuously available book.
  • Liquidity is unproven for NBA. Betfair's depth concentrates in Australian racing and international soccer. NBA in-play depth in Australian hours is an open question and needs the same Phase 1 survey Rev 1 specifies, with the same disqualification threshold.
  • API approval. Access requires a separate application, so factor the lead time into the roadmap rather than discovering it at Phase 2.

What this does to the roadmap

Phase 0 is entirely venue-independent. Whether PW carries information beyond the market price is the question that decides this project, and it is answered from historical data with no account, no API key and no capital at either venue. Run it first and let the venue decision wait — if the answer is negative, both the jurisdiction problem and the commission comparison become moot.

If the answer is positive, the venue choice is a straightforward trade: Betfair costs modestly more per trade, removes the maker-side optimisation, adds a success penalty, and has unproven basketball depth — against which it removes the eligibility question entirely, eliminates custody and bridge risk, allows the bot to run beside the monitor with no VPS, and dissolves the compliance boundary described below.

On the proxy question

The note mentions a Malaysia-based proxy. Hosting infrastructure in a country you are physically in is simply hosting. Interposing infrastructure so that activity presents as originating somewhere other than where the operator belongs is the thing Rev 1 correctly rejected on terms-of-service grounds, and it does not change character according to who owns the proxy. Given the residency position above, the hosting layer is not where this question gets resolved, and no configuration of servers resolves it.

The AU server is now a compliance boundary

Rev 1's design principle 4 — "the trading bot is a standalone service; it does not read monitor state files" — was written as separation of concerns. With an Australian monitor host it is doing more work than that, and it should be enforced mechanically rather than left as convention:

  • The AU host holds no wallet, no signing key and no CLOB credentials.
  • The AU host makes no requests to Polymarket endpoints, including the public Gamma API. Enforce with an egress allowlist, not a code review.
  • Signal flow is one-directional and carries game data only — no order state, no balances, no positions.
  • P&L views on the AU host, if wanted, are served from data pushed by the bot rather than pulled from the platform.

The pull-based transport in Section 09 fits this cleanly: the bot reaches into the monitor for signals, and nothing reaches out from Australia. Note that this is good hygiene and good architecture — it is not an answer to the residency question, and it should not be mistaken for one.

Plan the wind-down before the build

The temporary posting has an end date, and on return both eligibility limbs are engaged. That makes an orderly exit a scheduled requirement rather than a contingency, and it is far easier to design now than to improvise later with open positions:

  • Stop opening positions at a defined lead time before the return date, sized so that the longest-dated position settles before departure.
  • Test the withdrawal path early and repeatedly. An untested withdrawal route is a liability precisely when it is needed, and account status can change faster than a settlement cycle.
  • Do not carry the account across the transition on the assumption it will keep working. Plan for the position to close, not to pause.

Tax

The earlier revision pointed this at a Malaysian accountant. With Australian residence retained, that is likely wrong. Australia taxes residents on worldwide income, and someone temporarily abroad commonly remains an Australian tax resident under the domicile test unless a permanent place of abode has been established overseas — which "temporarily" tends to argue against. The relevant questions are therefore Australian ones, and there are two:

  • Residency status for the period abroad, which is fact-specific and worth confirming rather than assuming.
  • Characterisation of the gains. Australia generally does not tax gambling winnings, but does tax business income, and a systematic automated strategy run continuously to a defined method is a strong fact pattern for carrying on a business. The answer determines whether gains are assessable and whether losses are deductible — and the two questions are linked, so a favourable answer on one is often unfavourable on the other.

On the capital at risk

Worth stating plainly, because the engineering can obscure it: the base rate for retail systems of this kind is negative expected value after costs, and Rev 1's own analysis suggests why — PW fires on teams that are already visibly ahead, which is exactly the information a live market has already priced. The 98% backfill contamination means the historical ROI figures provide little evidence against that.

The right frame for the whole project is that its primary output is an answer, not a return. The architecture should be optimised to discover cheaply and quickly that there is no edge, if that is the truth. That is why this review moves the decisive analysis to Phase 0, where it costs nothing, and why it treats the paper phase as a search for reasons to stop rather than a hurdle to clear. Position sizing should stay small enough that the answer, whichever it turns out to be, is affordable.
